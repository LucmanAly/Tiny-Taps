# Baby Boo — franchise bible v1.2
Owner: Luqman Ali. Prepared 2026-10-24.

## What is established
The four approved stock designs, the lavender jungle house, and Episode 2: Hide and Seek are the current foundation. The character sheet and completed Episode 2 were accepted in the originating conversation. This kit preserves those identities and exports reusable assets from their drawing code.

“Established” means supported by those approvals, Episode 2, or subsequent explicit owner instructions. The personality guidance below is a working creative convention. New location concepts and future episode ideas remain proposals; their inclusion does not make their events part of the story history.

## The promise
A small, cozy nighttime world where a little ghost and his purple friend discover things together, feel ordinary feelings, and help each other. A child should finish an episode feeling curious, comforted and connected. The default audience is roughly ages 2–5, centered on age 3. The franchise spans short videos and simple touch games.

The monster is a friend, not a villain or Boo's sibling. Mommy and Daddy have no established personal names, occupations, new powers or separate origin stories. Do not add them as facts. These are whimsical ghost characters; the series does not need an explanation involving death or an afterlife.

## Recurring candy trait
Owner-confirmed on 2026-10-24: Purple Monster loves candies and is always keen to have them. Wanting candy is a recurring character trait; Baby Boo gives him candies. Carry this detail into new stories and game design while preserving their warm friendship. This clarification does not supply Episode 1's missing plot or establish any particular candy adventure as a completed episode.

## Scale and modeling
Daddy remains tallest; Mommy is somewhat shorter; Purple Monster's body is near Mommy's size and his horns add height; Boo is distinctly smaller. Use the approved lineup and renderer multipliers. The multipliers are drawing scales, not direct silhouette-height percentages:
- Daddy 1.00000
- Mommy 0.86885
- Baby Boo 0.52459
- Purple Monster 0.83607

Apply a shared scene scale to the group. Perspective may affect an individual scale when their depth visibly changes. Keep their relative proportions stable when they stand at the same depth. Retain the same eyes, curl, bow locations, horns, fur edge and ghost hem across shots. Do not add permanent hats, outfits, extra horns or a bow to Boo without a deliberate design decision.

## Home base
A small lavender wooden house in a gentle jungle clearing. It has horizontal boards, a triangular roof with scalloped purple shingles, a round warm-yellow window on the left, a warm-yellow rectangular window with a cross mullion on the right, and an arched brown door. Lavender stepping stones lead home. Palms frame the left; rounded green bushes and a pink mushroom sit on the right. Smiling pumpkins and little yellow-green fireflies provide warm light.

Outside, a dark purple sky, soft purple clouds, twinkling stars, a big orange moon with subtle craters, and small dark bats establish the world. The orange moon is normally at upper right in the standard home composition. A new camera angle may change its screen position; avoid accidental moon teleportation within a continuous shot.

The style is Halloween-inspired all year; every story does not have to be about Halloween. Do not turn the house into a new building whenever the scenery changes. The interior concept is a proposed room, not an approved architectural floor plan.

## Magic and world rules
- Boo's glow is established. It is warm, gentle and helpful, not a weapon or a flashing strobe.
- Episode 2 says that little ghosts can glow, but does not establish every adult's abilities. This kit gives the glow action to Boo only; it does not give Purple Monster or the parents new powers.
- Ghosts float gently. Purple Monster moves with small, readable footfalls.
- Fireflies behave like friendly little lights; they can gather into a heart as shown in Episode 2.
- Ordinary needs and emotions drive the stories. Add magic sparingly and label new rules as proposals.
- Being scared once does not define a character forever. Fear can recur and be met with patience.

## Story grammar
Begin with a familiar activity, introduce one small discovery or manageable problem, allow the friends to try, give one warm helping action, and close with reassurance or a happy return. Repetition and a short refrain help recognition. Let the children contribute to the solution; parents support without taking over every episode.

Use concrete words, short clauses, and a slow pace. Prefer a story that demonstrates kindness to a lecture about it. One episode can support one light learning goal: counting a few objects, identifying colors, listening, turn-taking, asking for help or naming a feeling. Do not cram several lessons into two minutes.

## Emotional boundaries
The audience sees friendly creatures, soft darkness and small uncertainties. Avoid jump scares, threatening chases, cruelty, graphic injury, aggressive teeth, humiliation and loud sudden effects. Show the cause of a surprise when useful, and resolve distress gently. A worried zigzag mouth is an expression, not a permanent redesign.

## Expansion
The lavender house remains the anchor. A firefly grove, quiet pond and cozy room are supplied as first location concepts. Connect a new setting using familiar colors, flora, fireflies, recurring props or a short journey from home. New recurring characters, lasting powers and major relationship changes need to be recognized as new creative decisions. A one-off prop or background can be drafted freely within the style.

## Canon discipline
Use CONTINUITY.md for accepted events and unanswered questions. Keep proposals in a separate section. After an accepted episode, record its title, central event, new approved assets, any world rule it establishes, and file versions. Never treat unused brainstorming as something the characters already did.

# Baby Boo — visual guide v1.0

## Authority
The approved four-character sheet, existing renderer and completed Episode 2 are the visual foundation. `references/Approved_Character_Sheet.png` is the original accepted sheet. `references/Cast_Reference.png` presents the same models with expression examples. Newly exported expressions and motion states are working production variants of those models, not replacements.

## Shape and finish
Rounded 2D vector-style silhouettes; clean curves; soft color shading; light outlines on white ghosts; a darker purple fuzzy outline on Monster; broad uncomplicated props. Render at 2x resolution and downsample with Lanczos. Keep the look readable at tablet size. Avoid photorealism, a 3D render look, busy fabric textures, hard metallic shading and thin noisy detail.

Ghosts have continuous rounded bodies with a wavy bottom edge and small rounded arms. Faces sit in consistent positions. The approved front view is supplied; full profile and rear models have not been approved or produced. A horizontal mirror is a temporary facing shortcut and mirrors Mommy's bow and Boo's curl; it is not a true opposite-facing model sheet.

## Composition
Give faces and hands clear space. Keep a calm foreground where a toddler can identify what to tap. Use a few strong props rather than many competing details. Camera moves should reveal a place or follow an action; avoid constant motion without a purpose. For a new place retain two or three familiar cues, such as palms, lavender stones, rounded bushes and warm fireflies.

## Lighting
The moon and windows are warm. Boo's glow has a white center and a pale-yellow soft halo. The surrounding night stays purple and green. Dim the world gradually when a cloud hides the moon. Leave important expressions readable. Glow should breathe gently; avoid large abrupt brightness changes.

## Movement
Use eased starts/stops and curved travel paths. Ghosts bob and lean slightly; Monster breathes, blinks, and alternates feet when traveling. Arms move through intermediate positions. Keep hands together during a hand-holding action; use shared contact coordinates instead of separate unrelated bobs. Stagger blinks and secondary motion. Finish an action before introducing the next attention cue.

## Reference statuses
- Approved base: original character sheet, core cast identities, home exterior and Episode 2 visual language.
- Production variants: exported expression poses and animation frames derived from the same code.
- Proposed settings: Firefly Grove, Moonlit Pond and Cozy Room. These are not Episode 1 history or an approved map.

# Baby Boo — story, video and game production

## Start each task
Read the current bible and continuity log; identify the relevant stock assets. Separate an established fact from a proposed addition. State the deliverable and its scope. Reuse approved assets without restarting character design.

## Episode outline template
Working title / canon or proposal / one emotional or learning goal / setting / cast / eight or fewer story beats / exact dialogue / rough timing / new assets required / ending. A useful beat pattern is: familiar hello; invitation; first attempt; small uncertainty; ask/listen; kind action; shared success; comforting goodbye. Vary it when the story benefits.

## Video workflow
1. Establish the script, actions and approximate timings. Let the dialogue be slow enough for a parent to read to a 3-year-old. Allow at least one second after each line. Around two minutes is a target, not a reason to rush.
2. Use the stock cast and approved home assets. For a substantially new recurring design, show its model sheet before animating it. For routine new scenery, create a reversible concept consistent with the palette.
3. Plan shots, lighting and entrances/exits so positions and props carry across cuts.
4. Render one scene per run to its own MP4. Default delivery is 1280x720 at 24 fps. Use the procedural drawing source for smooth intermediate motion rather than enlarging a low-resolution sprite animation for the whole film.
5. Inspect early, middle and final frames; check important actions such as hidden curl, covered eyes and joined hands. Join clips with gentle transitions; keep the exact final timing in one timeline file.
6. Deliver a voice-free version with optional original soft music, a fully silent version, SRT subtitles and a plain timestamp–speaker–line text file. If real narration is supplied later, retime captions and pauses against that actual recording.
7. Verify duration, dimensions, frame rate, decoding, caption timing, transitions and final download files. Record accepted story additions in CONTINUITY.md.

Do not add robotic narration or claim natural speech exists when it does not. Image generation and Sora are outside the current code-only production preference unless the owner changes that instruction.

## Game workflow
Decide the intended device, engine and input before a full build. For an early prototype, choose a small playable activity with one clear goal and one or two characters. Keep the same cast and palette as the videos.

Prefer large touch targets, generous hit areas, forgiving drag/drop, obvious feedback, optional sound, an easy replay and a clear way home. Avoid precision tapping, required reading, punitive failure and distracting simultaneous objectives. Keep interface labels outside sprite art. Save progress locally if that is all the game needs.

Use a stable entity anchor; changing animation should not move the character's position. Load atlas rectangles and per-frame durations from the manifest. Move entities with smooth paths independently of the sprite-frame rate. Use a separate glow overlay for flexible intensity. On state changes, complete or blend gestures when useful; do not crossfade whole character silhouettes so strongly that two bodies are visible.

For browser/tablet prototypes, check the actual touch layout, orientation, mute behavior and performance. Only claim a prototype is tested after running it. Do not turn a request for a game idea into a full website deployment without the requested scope.

## Paired content
A video can introduce a feeling or action; a small game can let the child repeat it. Examples: counting pumpkins after a counting story, tapping friendly fireflies after the glow story, or arranging a bedtime room after a goodnight story. Reuse assets while giving the game a real interaction.

## Versioning
Use stable character IDs, version new art packs, and retain accepted previous assets. A new setting is a concept until adopted. After an approved change, update the bible, log and relevant reference image together so future chats do not receive contradictory sources.
